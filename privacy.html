Your privacy

This site collects nothing about you. Not your name, not your birthday, not your address, not your visit.

In plain words

  • There are no forms on this site. There is nowhere to type personal information, because we never ask for any.
  • There are no accounts and no logins.
  • There are no cookies and no tracking of any kind.
  • The page does remember which state you picked, on your own device only. It is explained in full below.
  • It also remembers **which language you chose**, in the same way.
  • There is no analytics. We do not count visitors, and we do not know you were here.
  • There are no ads and no advertising pixels.
  • There is nothing sent to any other company. The fonts and the code are stored on this site itself, so your browser never contacts Google, a CDN, or anyone else while the page loads.

Because none of that exists, there is no cookie banner to click and no consent to give. There is nothing to consent to.

The one thing the site remembers

So that you do not have to start again every time, the page saves two short pieces of information on your own device: the state you picked, and whether you said you were signed up or not. When you come back, it takes you straight to where you were.

That is the whole list. For example: {"state":"OH","branch":"no"}. There is no name, no date of birth, no address, and nothing that could identify you.

It is not a cookie, and that matters

A cookie is attached to every request your browser makes to a website, so it travels to whoever runs the server. This uses something different, called local storage, which never leaves your browser. We do not receive it, cannot read it, and could not hand it over if we were asked. That is also why this site has no cookie banner — there is no cookie to ask you about.

How to clear it

There is a button for it. Once you have picked a state, scroll to the bottom of your results and press "Forget the state I picked". If the page greeted you with "Welcome back", there is a "Not you? Start over" button right there too.

It is also cleared whenever you clear your browsing data, and it is never there at all in a private or incognito window.

On a shared or public computer — a library, a school, a friend's laptop — press one of those buttons when you finish, so the next person does not see which state you were looking at.

The web address

When you pick your state, the two-letter code is also added to the address in your browser bar — for example #OH. That is so you can bookmark your state or send the link to someone. It happens inside your browser and is not sent to us. Your browser's own history holds it, exactly as it holds every other page you visit.

Why we designed it this way

Your full name, your date of birth, and your address are, together, the exact set of details someone needs to impersonate you or open credit in your name. A website that collects all three is worth attacking. The safest way to protect that information is to never hold it.

It also means we could not sell, leak, or be forced to hand over your data even if someone asked. There is nothing to hand over.

When you click through to your state

The official state pages we link to are run by state governments, not by us. Once you click one of those links, you are on their website and their privacy policy applies, not ours.

Those state pages will ask for your name and date of birth. That is normal and expected — it is the government's own system, and it is the only way it can find your voter record. It is not the same as an unknown website asking for the same details.

We send no referrer information with those links, so the state site is not told which page you came from.

Hosting

Whoever hosts these files may keep standard server logs, which typically include IP addresses. That is a property of web hosting in general, not something this site sets up or uses. This site's own code stores nothing.

Children

This site collects no information from anyone, including anyone under 13.
